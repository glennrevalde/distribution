This chapter covers what makes the system a distribution system: buying from suppliers by allocation and pick-up, receiving stock in crates whose weight varies, following every crate until it is sold, and selling by the crate or by the kilo out of a crate. The accounting behind it (item receipts, bills, sales, cost of sales) is the same as in the rest of the system.

How the distribution flow works

  1. Supplier Items lists what each supplier sells you, in which unit, at what cost.
  2. Supplier Allocation records what a supplier has set aside for you on a date, for a location: so many crates, kilos or packs of each item.
  3. Pick-Up Supplier Items is what the pick-up crew works from at the supplier: each crate is weighed and typed in as it is loaded.
  4. Pick-up complete receives the goods into the location on an Item Receipt. Every crate becomes an open crate with its own number, weight, heads, production date and expiry date.
  5. The supplier's invoice is entered later with Enter Bill for Received Items, as for any item receipt.
  6. Crates are sold whole, or kilos are taken out of a crate, on Create Invoices, Enter Sales Receipts and the POS Register. They can be moved to another site, and converted to loose kilos.
  7. Dispatch Inventory sends stock to the vans and outlets: crates go whole, picked by the figure the scale reads, and loose kilos can be put into new crates on the way out with Kilos to Crates.
  8. The Crate Center and the crate reports show every crate on hand, how much is left in it, and what was sold from it.
Who does what

As delivered, the Allocation Officer keeps the allocations and supplier items, the Pick-Up Crew sees only the pick-ups, the Inventory Clerk has the Crate Center, Dispatch Inventory, conversions both ways, moves and crate reports, and the Sales Representative sells in crates. Page Access Configuration can change any of this.

The crate unit of measure

An item is kept in crates when its U/M set has a crate unit. Open Inventory > Unit of Measure, open the set (for example Kilos and crates), add a unit such as CRT and tick Varies (crate). The factor box is then switched off: a crate holds whatever it weighed when it was received, so the system never converts crates to kilos by a fixed number. A set can have one crate unit.

The base unit of the set is what a crate is measured in: kilos for meat and poultry, packs for packed goods (for example a crate of 40 packs of bola-bola). Stock, costs and prices stay in the base unit; the crate count is kept beside it. A line in crates shows both, for example 2 CRT = 25.32 kg.

Supplier Items

Where to find it: Vendors > Distribution > Supplier Items.

For each supplier, the items it sells you, the unit it allocates them in (crates, kilos or packs), the supplier's own code for the item and the last cost. The list fills the allocation form when Go is pressed, in the order set here, and the last cost is the cost of the item receipt made at pick-up. Each pick-up updates the last cost.

Supplier Allocation

Where to find it: Vendors > Distribution > Supplier Allocation. The list shows every allocation with its date, supplier, location, who picks it up and its status; filter it by date, supplier, location and status.

Recording an allocation

  1. Press New allocation.
  2. Choose the Supplier, the Date of the pick-up and the Location the goods are received into, then press Go. The supplier's items are listed.
  3. Choose who picks it up under Assigned to (a user, who then sees it under Only mine), or type a driver or helper who is not a user.
  4. Type the Quantity allocated of each item in its unit. A line with a quantity is ticked under Use; untick a line to leave it out. Copy repeats a line (the same item in another unit), the bin removes it, and Add another item adds an item the supplier list does not have.
  5. Press Save (or Save & New).
Button What it does

Save, Save & New Saves the allocation; Save & New starts another one for the same date and location.

Pick-Up Supplier Items

Where to find it: Vendors > Distribution > Pick-Up Supplier Items. It is laid out for a phone, since the crew uses it at the supplier.

The page shows the pick-ups of one day; the arrows beside the date move a day back or forward. Only the pick-ups assigned to me hides everyone else's. Each supplier is a card with its location, who picks it up, the allocation number and a progress line (items done, crates weighed). Tap a card to open it.

Weighing crates

  1. Press Weigh crates on an item in crates. The crate window opens with one row for each crate allocated.
  2. Type each crate's Weight as it comes off the scale; Enter moves to the next crate. Type the Heads (birds or pieces) when they are counted.
  3. Type the Heads, Production date and Expiry date on the first crate: they are copied at once to the crates below it. Each crate whose weight you type passes its heads and dates on to the next one, so the whole load usually needs them typed once. The weight itself is never copied. Any copied value can still be changed; a value you change is that crate's own and is passed on to the crates under it from then on. Replicate copies the first crate's heads and dates to every crate again, and Add crates starts new rows with the heads and dates of the last crate.
  4. The crate numbers are given in sequence; change one to match the number painted on the crate if needed. Add crates adds more rows when more crates were loaded than allocated; the x removes a crate.
  5. Press Save crates. The scale readout at the bottom shows the total weight and the number of crates.

An item allocated by the kilo or by the pack has a box for the quantity picked up and Save instead. The circle at the right of an item is ticked when it is done. Reset clears what was picked up on that card.

Completing a pick-up

When every item is done, choose the receiving date and press Pick-up complete. The system enters an Item Receipt for the supplier into the location, at the last cost from Supplier Items, with every crate on it. The allocation becomes Received and links to the receipt. Enter the supplier's invoice later with Enter Bill for Received Items.

Voiding the receipt

Voiding or deleting an item receipt made by a pick-up sets the allocation back to Being picked up with its weights, so it can be completed again. It is refused while any of its crates has already been sold or moved; void or change that document first.

Receiving crates without an allocation

Receive Items, Receive Items and Enter Bills, Enter Bills and Write Checks receive crates too. Choose the item, choose the crate unit (for example CRT) on the line, and the same crate window opens; type the crates and save. The line's quantity becomes the total kilos of the crates and its rate is per kilo. A line in the crate unit cannot be saved without its crates: the message says press Crates on the line. The crate button on the line shows how many crates it holds and opens the window again to change them.

The crate window copies the heads, production date and expiry date down the rows here too, exactly as when weighing a pick-up; only the weight is typed for every crate.

Two open crates can never have the same number. A crate received on a document can be changed or removed only while nothing has been sold from it.

Selling in crates and by the kilo

Create Invoices, Enter Sales Receipts and the POS Register sell crates in two ways:

  • Whole crates. Choose the crate unit on the line. The crate window lists the open crates of the item at the site with their weight left, heads, production and expiry dates (the ones expiring within three days or expired are marked). Type Crates needed and press Oldest first, or tick the crates yourself; find a crate by its number. Each crate goes whole, and the line is priced per kilo on the total weight.
  • Kilos out of a crate. Sell the item in kilos and press the crate button on the line. Tick the crate it comes from and type the kilos under Take; a sale can take from several crates.

What is taken out is deducted from the crate. A crate of 12.87 kg from which 6 kg is sold has 6.87 kg left and stays open; when the rest is sold the crate is closed as Sold and leaves the crates on hand. The document shows and prints, under each line, the crates it took with their kilos and expiry dates.

Stock in crates is sold by the crate

Stock received in crates is sold by the crate. A sale line that names no crate may only take the loose stock of the site: stock that is not in any open crate (stock received by the kilo, or crates converted to kilos). If it asks for more, it is refused and the message says how much is loose. Press the crate button to choose the crate, sell by the crate, or convert crates first. The items of an item group sold together are not held to this rule.

At the POS Register

At the register, choosing an item whose sales unit is the crate, or typing more kilos than are loose, opens the crate window. The cart shows a crate button on the line with the number of crates; tap it to change them. A suspended sale keeps its crates; a recalled sale brings them back, and a voided or returned sale puts them back.

Voids, edits and deletes

Editing a sale gives back the crates it no longer takes; voiding or deleting it puts every kilo back into its crates, and a crate closed by the sale opens again.

Crate Center

Where to find it: Inventory > Crates > Crate Center.

The top lists the crates by item and site: how many, what is in them, the average per crate and the earliest expiry. Below is every crate, oldest expiry first, with its number, heads, what it was received with, what is left (the bar shows how full it is), the dates, the supplier and the status. Expiry dates within three days are marked, expired ones in red. Filter by site, item, status (Open, Sold out, Used up, Converted or all), expiry (within 3, 7, 15 or 30 days, or expired) and crate number.

Tick crates and press Convert ticked to kilos or Move ticked to go to those pages with the crates chosen. Click a crate number to open its sheet: what it holds, its dates and how many days are left, where it came from, and its whole history (received, sold, moved, adjusted up or down, taken by an adjustment, converted, shrinkage, shortage charged). A crate emptied by an adjustment, as shrinkage or as a shortage, is Used up rather than sold.

Convert Crates to Kilos

Where to find it: Inventory > Crates > Convert Crates to Kilos.

Converting a crate stops following it crate by crate: what is left in it stays on hand as loose kilos (or packs) and is sold by the kilo. Nothing is posted and the stock on hand does not change. Tick the crates, type a note if wanted and press Convert. The conversion is in the audit trail and on the crate's history.

Convert Kilos to Crates

Where to find it: Inventory > Crates > Convert Kilos to Crates.

The other way round from Convert Crates to Kilos: loose stock of an item kept in crates (kilos or packs that are in no open crate) is put into new crates where it is, so that from then on it is followed and sold crate by crate. Nothing is posted to the books; the quantity and the value on hand stay the same. To fill crates as the stock leaves for a van or outlet, do it on Dispatch Inventory instead.

  1. Choose the Site. The table lists every item kept in crates there with its On hand, how many crates it is In, and how much is Loose.
  2. Press Kilos to Crates (Packs to Crates for an item counted in packs) on the item. An item with nothing loose shows Nothing loose.
  3. In the window, choose the production / expiry group the kilos come from (see Production and expiry date groups), then type each crate's Dispatch figure as the scale reads it: 1330 is 13.30 kg. Enter goes to the next crate. The crate numbers follow on from the last crate of the company; typing over a number carries on from it (Kilos to Crates has the details).
  4. Press Save crates. The Ready to convert panel shows the item, the total on a scale readout, the group and the crate numbers. Check the Date, type a Note if wanted (it is kept in each crate's history) and press Convert to crates. Change opens the window again.

The new crates are open crates like any received one: they show in the Crate Center and the crate reports, and their history starts with Filled from loose stock. The conversion is in the audit trail.

Stock without dates given dates in the window takes those dates as well: the kilos move from the stock without dates to the dates typed, recorded as a date change on the Adjustments list (nothing is posted to the books). The message after the conversion names it; voiding it takes the dates and the crates back.

Move Crates to Another Site

Where to find it: Inventory > Crates > Move Crates to Another Site.

Choose the site the crates are at and the site they go to, tick the crates and press Move. The system enters an inventory transfer of their kilos, and the crates keep their numbers, weights and dates at the new site. Voiding the transfer brings them back, unless a crate has been sold or moved again since.

Transferring crates to outlets and van stores

Where to find it: Inventory > Transfer Inventory.

Stock kept in crates goes to an outlet or a van store by the crate, so each crate keeps its number, heads, weight and dates at the place it goes to, and is followed there the same way: sold whole, sold by the kilo, or brought back at the closing.

  1. Choose the From site (for example Naga Cold Storage) and the To site (the outlet or van store).
  2. Choose the item on a line. When the item has open crates at the source site, the Crates button on the line can be pressed.
  3. Press Crates. The crate window lists the open crates of the item at the source site, with their weight, heads, production and expiry dates, and shows how much of the item is loose there. Type Crates needed and press Oldest first, or tick the crates yourself; find one by its number, or by its weight in Find figure (see Find figure).
  4. Press Save crates. The button shows how many crates were chosen and Qty to move becomes their total weight; it can only be changed through the crate window.
  5. Press Record transfer.

Every quantity on the form carries its unit after it (88.12 kg, 274 pack). When the item has two units or more, holding the pointer over the quantity (or tapping it on a phone) shows what it is made of: the crates and the loose stock of On hand at source, the crates chosen on the line, or the quantity typed in its other units, for example = 2.25 box (20 pack each).

The saved transfer has one line for each crate, shown and printed with its crate number and kilos, for example 1 CRT = 12.87 kg. Editing the transfer can change the crates; voiding or deleting it brings every crate back to the source site, unless one of them has been sold or moved again since.

Kilos without a crate

A line of an item kept in crates that names no crate may only move the loose stock of the source site (stock not in any open crate). If it asks for more, the transfer is refused and the message says how much is loose there, for example: WNG-FZ Chicken Wings, Frozen, Bag is kept in crates at Naga Cold Storage: 0 kg of it is loose, and this transfer moves 500 kg without naming a crate. Press Crates on the line, or convert crates to kilos first.

Printing the transfer for the van: the POS receipt

A saved transfer prints on a POS roll printer (58 or 80 mm), on a Bluetooth roll printer, or as a PDF to send, so the van salesman or the outlet knows exactly which crates he is carrying.

  1. Open the transfer from the list and press Print POS Receipt.
  2. The slip shows the company, the transfer number and date, the From and To sites, the reference and the memo. The body lists Crate#, Item Code and Qty/Unit: one row for each crate with the weight it went with (for example 11.65kg), and one row for what was moved without a crate, in the unit it was entered in (for example 24pack). The crate number is printed in bold and a size larger; only the item code is printed, to save paper.
  3. Under each item code, a Subtotal gives its crates and its kilos (or packs), for example Subtotal FMWC: 9 crates, 112.35kg, when the slip lists more than one item. The Total gives the number of crates and the total of each unit, then the number of lines. The footer has Transferred by: and Received by: with a line to sign over the names (the person who entered the transfer and the van salesman or cashier answerable for the site it went to).
  4. Press Print to send it to a POS printer installed on the computer or phone, Bluetooth printer to print on the paired roll printer, or Save as PDF to keep or send a soft copy.

Group by above the slip lays it out in one of two ways; Print, the Bluetooth printer and Save as PDF follow the one chosen:

  • Item code (as it opens): the crates and lines of each item together, a subtotal under each item. On a dispatch from more than one site, each row carries the code of the site it left beside the item code (FMWC NGA), with the site's marker.
  • Site: the site's code and name as a heading, what left that site under it item by item, and a subtotal per site.

The paper width (58 or 80 mm) and the Bluetooth printer are set in Company, Security & Closing Date. The Bluetooth printer button is shown only when it is switched on there. A saved Inventory Transfer Closing has the same button: it prints the transfer that took the stock back.

Dispatch Inventory

Where to find it: Inventory > Dispatch Inventory (opens the list of dispatches; press New dispatch). Also the Dispatch button of the Inventory Center, Dispatch inventory on the Inventory Site List, and Dispatch Inventory at the top of Transfer Inventory.

Dispatch Inventory sends stock from the cold storage or warehouse to a van store or outlet, as Transfer Inventory does, with what the dispatch counter needs: crates on hand are picked by the figure the scale shows, and loose kilos can be put into new crates as they go out, without converting them first. A dispatch is numbered DSP-, moves the stock from the From site to the To site at average cost, and posts nothing to the general ledger. One dispatch can gather stock from several sites into one van or outlet (From many sites to one).

  1. Check the Date, tick the From sites (one, for example Naga Cold Storage, or several) and choose the To site (the van or outlet).
  2. On each line choose the Item, or press Add beside it in Stock at the sites; with two or more From sites, choose under the item the site it comes from. On hand at source shows the quantity at that site with its unit; hold the pointer over it to see how much is in crates and how much is loose.
  3. Choose the Conversion of the line: As kept sends the stock as it is (the crates on hand, or a quantity of an item not kept in crates); Kilos to Crates (Packs to Crates) puts loose stock into new crates on the way out.
  4. For crates on hand, press Crates and pick them (Find figure). For Kilos to Crates, the window opens by itself (Kilos to Crates). For an item not kept in crates, type the Qty to dispatch.
  5. Check Total to dispatch under the lines and press Record dispatch (or Record dispatch & New).

From many sites to one

Stock for one van or outlet often sits in more than one place: whole chicken at the cold storage, nuggets at the depot. From sites takes them all on one dispatch, and everything arrives at the one To site.

  1. Press From sites and tick each site the stock comes from. Each has its own marker, a small coloured square, that follows it on every line, list and slip. All sites ticks every site, Only the first keeps the first. The To site cannot be ticked: it reads the To site.
  2. Each line then shows, under its item, the site it comes from with its marker. On hand at source, the production and expiry groups and the crates the line can send are those of that site.
  3. To send the same item from two sites, put it on two lines, one for each site.
  4. Under the lines, Total to dispatch gives the total, and above it what each site gives (From Legazpi Depot 24.53 kg (2 crates)).

Stock at the sites

Between the header and the lines, Stock at the sites lists what each site ticked holds, item by item: the Site with its marker, the item, On hand, In crates (kilos and the number of crates), Loose, and the Earliest expiry of what it holds with the days left (amber when it expires within three days, red once expired).

  • Type in Filter by item code or name to narrow the list: the same item at each site shows one row per site, each with its site's marker, so you see at once where the stock is.
  • Press a site's name above the list to hide its rows (its marker becomes an outline), and again to show them.
  • Add puts the item on the first empty line, from that site.
  • Hide folds the list away; the page remembers it on this browser.
Production and expiry dates

A line takes its date groups and its crates from its own site, and the stock arrives at the To site in the same dates; the saved dispatch shows them under each line. Transfers by Item Code and Transfers by Site list the dates each line went with.

Find figure: picking crates by their weight

At the dispatch counter each crate is put on the scale and the figure it shows is read out as a whole number, for example 1294 for 12.94 kg. The crate window of Dispatch Inventory and Transfer Inventory has a Find figure box for it:

  • Type the figure. As you type, only the crates whose weight starts with those digits stay on the list (7 crates next to the box says how many), so 12 leaves the crates of 12-point-something kg and 1294 the crate of 12.94 kg. A crate whose weight is exactly the figure is marked.
  • Press Enter to take the crate found (the exact one first). The box clears for the next crate, so a whole van load is picked by typing figure, Enter, figure, Enter.
  • A figure with a decimal point is taken as it is: 12.94 is 12.94 kg. For items counted in packs or pieces the figure is the count itself.

On Dispatch Inventory the window also has a Dispatch column: the figure of each crate as read at the counter. Typing a crate's figure there ticks the crate and shows the weight it stands for (= 12.41 kg); a figure that is not the crate's weight is marked red and the window cannot be saved until it is corrected or the crate is unticked, because a crate goes whole. The grey figure in an empty box is the one expected.

Kilos to Crates: filling crates on the way out

Loose stock (kilos, or packs, that are in no open crate) can be dispatched in new crates, so that the van sells it crate by crate. Choose Kilos to Crates under Conversion: the crate window opens to fill crates.

  1. The top shows the loose stock at the From site, by production and expiry group. Choose the group the kilos come from.
  2. Each row is a new crate. The Crate no. follows on from the last crate number of the company and can be typed over: the crates under it then carry on from the number typed, plus 1 each (type 900 and the next crates become 901, 902 and so on). A number already used by an open crate is refused.
  3. Type the Heads if wanted (it is copied down to the crates below), then the Dispatch figure as the scale reads it, as a whole number: 1330 becomes a Weight of 13.30 kg (1330 × .01). Enter goes to the next crate, and adds one at the end. The weight can also be typed itself.
  4. The scale readout at the bottom gives the total and the number of crates; Left loose in the group after shows what stays loose. More than the group holds cannot be saved.
  5. Press Save crates. The line shows how many crates and Qty to dispatch becomes their total. Rows left without a weight are dropped.

When the dispatch is recorded, each crate is filled at the From site (its history says Packed from loose stock) and moved to the To site with the rest. From then on it is an open crate like any received one: it keeps its number, heads, weight and dates, is sold whole or by the kilo, and comes back at the closing. The quantity is the same as before: the kilos left the loose stock and are now in crates, and the sale of the crate is still priced by the kilo (or pack) in it.

Production and expiry date groups

With Track inventory production date and expiry date switched on, the loose stock of an item that keeps dates is shown by group: one card for each production and expiry date the From site still holds loose, oldest first, with how much of it is loose. Choose the group to fill the crates from: the crates take its production and expiry dates, and the kilos come out of that group only.

Loose stock with no dates on file (or with date tracking off) is one group, No production or expiry date. For it, the window has Production date and Expiry date boxes: the dates typed there go on every crate filled, and on the kilos in it. The kilos leave the cold storage from the stock without dates and arrive at the van or outlet with the dates typed; the saved dispatch reads, for example, No dates to Prod. 11/21/2026, Exp. 05/20/2027 under the line.

When some of the kilos without dates came in with dates on their documents (version 5's Put in agreement with the crates moved them there), the window says how many under the group, with a link to Take the dates from the documents. Put them back first, then open the window again: they show in the group of their own dates.

What a dispatch records

  • One line for each crate, with the crate unit and its kilos (1 CRT = 13.3 kg), noted packed for a crate filled on the dispatch; one line for each other item. A dispatch from more than one site shows From sites with their markers, and a From site column on its lines; the list of dispatches names every site.
  • The stock of each line moves from its site to the To site at average cost, with its production and expiry dates; nothing is posted to the general ledger.
  • Print POS Receipt prints the slip for the van on the roll printer, as a PDF or on a Bluetooth printer, as for a transfer (the POS receipt): titled INVENTORY DISPATCH, one row per crate with the crate number in bold, and Dispatched by: and Received by: to sign.
  • A dispatch that moved or filled crates is not edited: void it and record it again. Voiding it brings the crates back to the sites they came from, and the crates it filled are taken apart again into loose stock, unless one of them has been sold or moved since; the message then says which.
Loose kilos of an item kept in crates

A line of an item kept in crates sent As kept without naming crates may only take the loose stock of the From site. If it asks for more, the dispatch is refused and the message says how much is loose there: press Crates on the line to send the crates themselves, or dispatch less of the loose stock.

Who may dispatch is set on Page Access Configuration (Dispatch Inventory, under Inventory). As delivered, the roles that transfer inventory dispatch it too.

Inventory Transfer Closing

Where to find it: Inventory > Inventory Transfer Closing.

At the end of a trip the van salesman remits his sales and brings back what was not sold. The closing lists everything the van (or an outlet) still holds, records what came back to the warehouse or cold storage, and puts every difference down as shrinkage, as a shortage, or as part of each. The list page shows every closing with its van or outlet, the site the stock went back to, the person, the lines, the shrinkage and the shortage; filter it by date, site and person.

Closing a van or outlet

  1. Press New closing. Choose From (the van store or outlet being closed), To (the warehouse or cold storage it goes back to) and the Closing date, then press Show balances.
  2. Every item the site still holds is listed with its Balance: one line for each open crate (with its number, heads and expiry date), and one line for the loose stock of each item. Stock below zero is shown but cannot be closed.
  3. Van salesman, sales rep or cashier is filled with the person answerable for the site (set on the Inventory Site List). Shortage charged to follows the person: the employee as a customer. Another customer can be chosen. A person not on the list yet is added from the box itself with Add new sales representative.
  4. Check the Shortage receivable GL account (1120 Shortage Receivable - Employees as delivered) and the Shrinkage adjustment GL account (5100 Inventory Adjustments and Shrinkage), and type who Transferred by, Checked by and Received by.
  5. Type in Transfer qty/wgt what came back: each crate's weight as re-weighed, or the kilos, packs or other unit counted. A line with a unit of measure set can be counted in any of its units; the balance follows the unit chosen. All came back fills every ticked line with its balance.
  6. Untick a line that stays at the site.
  7. Press Save closing.

Shrinkage and shortage

The Variance of a line is its balance less what came back. It has to be put down as shrinkage, as shortage, or split between them, so that what came back, the shrinkage and the shortage add up to the balance. The form does the likely split as you type:

  • A crate that came back lighter dried out or lost drip: the difference goes to Shrinkage.
  • A crate that did not come back, or loose stock that came back short, is missing: the difference goes to Shortage.
  • Typing in Shrinkage puts the rest of the variance in Shortage, and the other way round. Variance to shrinkage and Variance to shortage put every difference on one side at once.

The foot of the form adds up what came back, the shrinkage and the shortage by unit, the shrinkage at cost (for users who see costs) and the shortage charged at cost. A line whose figures do not add up is marked red and the closing cannot be saved until it is put right.

What a closing records

Saving a closing enters up to three documents, all linked from the closing:

DocumentWhat it does
Inventory transfer (IT-)Moves what came back from the van to the warehouse. Crates go back whole with their numbers and dates, at the weight they came back with.
Inventory adjustment (ADJ-), reason ShrinkageTakes the shrinkage out of the van at average cost: debit the shrinkage adjustment account, credit Inventory.
Shortage Charge (SHC-)Takes the shortage out of the van at average cost and charges it to the person: debit the shortage receivable account, credit Inventory. It is a receivable of the employee as a customer, collected with Receive Payments. It is not a sale: no sales, no VAT and no cost of goods sold.

A crate taken down to nothing by shrinkage or a shortage is closed as Used up. The closing prints on one page with the three signature lines; Print POS Receipt prints its transfer back on the roll printer or as a PDF, crate by crate.

Voiding a closing

A closing is not edited; press Void closing and give the reason. Its transfer, shrinkage adjustment and shortage charge are voided with it and the stock is back at the van, to be closed again. A closing whose shortage has been paid, even in part, cannot be voided until that payment is voided. The documents a closing entered cannot be voided or edited on their own: the message says to void the closing instead.

Shortage Charges

Where to find it: Inventory > Shortage Charges.

Every shortage charged to a cashier, sales representative or van salesman, from a closing or from Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand, with its total and what is still open. The cards at the top show what each person still owes, with a Receive payment button. Click a number to open the charge: the items, the quantities and the cost charged, and the closing it came from.

Collecting a shortage

  1. Press Receive payment on the person's card, or open Customers > Receive Payments and choose the person in Received from. Employees are shown with what they are, for example Arnel Cruz (Van salesman), and the note under the box names the sales representative the shortages belong to.
  2. Their open shortage charges are listed with any invoices. Tick them, type the amount received, and save.

The payment credits the shortage receivable account of each charge. A shortage can also be deducted from pay outside the system and recorded the same way.

Adjusting the balances of a site

Two options of Inventory > Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand help with counts at an outlet or van:

  • Display Inventory Balances: tick it to put a line for every item the chosen site holds, or that is below zero there. Items at zero are left out. Remove the lines that are not adjusted with the red cross; lines left without a change are not saved anyway. Unticking it, or choosing another site, takes off the lines it added that were not changed.
  • Shortage charged to (cashier or sales rep): when stock is short because of a person, choose them. The reason becomes Shortage, and the decreases are charged to that person at average cost on a Shortage Charge instead of the adjustment account. Only decreases can be charged. A person not on the list is added from the box itself.

Adjusting stock crate by crate

Where to find it: Inventory > Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand.

An item kept in crates is adjusted crate by crate, so every crate keeps its own weight: you choose which crates change and by how much, and the system adds them up. The system does not spread a lump adjustment over the crates by itself.

  1. Choose the Inventory site, the Reason and the item on a line. When the item has open crates at the site, the Crates button on the line is marked.
  2. Press Crates. The window lists every open crate of the item at the site with its number, heads, production and expiry dates and the kilos it holds now (and what it came in with), and a last row for the Loose stock, not in any crate.
  3. For each crate that changed, type its New weight (what the scale says now) or its Adjust +/- (for example -0.35); the other fills in by itself. Crates left blank are not adjusted. All to zero takes every crate shown and the loose stock to 0; Clear empties what was typed; find a crate by its number.
  4. The readout at the bottom gives the total adjustment and how many crates changed. Press Save adjustment.
  5. The line shows how many crates were adjusted (for example 2 crates, or 2 + loose), and its New quantity and Qty difference are the total of the crates, in the item's base unit; they can only be changed through the window. Press Crates again to change them.
  6. Save the adjustment as usual.
  • A crate can go down to 0 (it is then closed as Used up) or back up to what it came in with, not more. A weight out of that range is marked red and cannot be saved.
  • Stock found that belongs to no crate goes on the Loose row. The loose stock can be lowered only down to what is loose at the site.
  • Set Qty to Zero takes an item in crates to 0 crate by crate, loose stock included.
  • Per Batch Adjustment has the same Crates button beside New qty for every item with open crates at the site.
  • With Shortage charged to, the crates are charged to the person on the Shortage Charge, crate by crate; a shortage can only lower them.
  • The saved adjustment shows its crates under the line, for example Crates 71 (-0.35 kg), 73 (-0.6 kg), and each crate's sheet in the Crate Center shows the adjustment in its history. Editing the adjustment opens the window with each crate as it was adjusted; voiding or deleting it puts the kilos back in the crates.
Lowering crated stock without naming a crate

A line that lowers an item kept in crates without pressing Crates may only lower its loose stock. If it lowers more, saving is refused and the message says how much is loose there, for example: ACU-FZ Chicken Assorted Cut-Ups, Frozen, Bag is kept in crates at Naga Cold Storage: 27.73 kg of it is loose, and this adjustment lowers it by 182.76 kg without naming a crate. Press Crates on the line and type the new weight of each crate adjusted.

Sales representatives, van salesmen and cashiers

The people kept on the Sales Rep list (Financial > Terms, Tax Codes & Other Lists) have a Kind: Sales representative, Van salesman or Cashier. Each is also kept as a customer of type Employee, made by the system, so that a shortage can be charged to them and collected like any receivable. Two people cannot have the same name on the list.

The Sales representative box of the customer form, the closing and the adjustment ends its list with Add new sales representative: the form opens in a window over the page and, once saved, the new person is chosen in the box without leaving the form. On the Inventory Site List, Van salesman or cashier answerable names the person a closing of that site starts with.

How crates stay in agreement with the stock

  • The kilos in a site's open crates can never be more than the item's stock on hand at that site.
  • Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand changes crates only as they are named in its crate window, each by its own amount; it never takes kilos from the crates by itself.
  • When stock leaves without naming crates in other ways (a physical count, a processing order or a build), the system takes the kilos from the open crates by itself, oldest expiry first, and records it on each crate as Taken by an adjustment.
  • A crate can be sold from only up to what it holds; the window refuses more.
  • Pending and suspended sales hold their crates; voids and deletes give them back.
  • Crates filled from loose stock (Convert Kilos to Crates, or Kilos to Crates on a dispatch) can never hold more than the loose stock of their date group at the site.
  • With production and expiry dates tracked, the stock in a crate carries the crate's own dates: crates received go each into the date group of its own dates (crates of several dates on one line are split by crate), and stock sold, moved, adjusted or charged from a crate leaves that crate's group. A line that names no crate takes the loose stock: in each date group, the kilos the open crates hold are left aside.
  • Stock taken out without naming a crate beyond the loose stock (a count, a processing order) comes first out of the crates of the date groups the stock no longer holds, then from the oldest crates.
  • Production and Expiry Dates shows any item whose crates carry dates the stock does not, and puts it right. It never takes dates off stock that has them: crates without dates that hold kilos the stock records with dates are given those dates (their history shows Dates given).
  • A transfer, a dispatch, an adjustment, a shrinkage adjustment and a shortage charge take whole or part crates as they are named; a closing checks, before it is saved, that every crate going back holds exactly what came back.

Crate reports

The crate reports are under Inventory > Reports > Crate Reports and in the Report Center; the allocation report is under Vendors > Reports > Distribution Reports. They print, export, save as PDF and can be modified like any other report.

Report What it shows
Crates Remaining by Item The open crates of each item and site: how many, the kilos (or packs) in them, the average per crate, the earliest expiry and the days left. Tick Show the loose kilos to add the stock not in crates and the total on hand.
Kilos per Crate by Item Every crate of each item, with the kilos received and left, its dates, days left, supplier and status.
Crates Sold with Production and Expiry Dates What was sold from each crate: the document, the customer, whole or part, the kilos, and the production and expiry dates, with the days to expiry when it was sold.
Crate Movement History Every crate movement in a period: received, sold, moved, adjusted, taken by an adjustment, converted, shrinkage and shortages.
Crates Received by Supplier The crates received from each supplier: crates, heads, kilos, the average per crate and the cost.
Supplier Allocation vs Pick-Up For each allocation, what was allocated and what was picked up, the difference, and the kilos.

Transfer reports

Under Inventory > Reports > Transfer Reports and in the Report Center. Both list the stock sent by Transfer Inventory (numbers IT-) and Dispatch Inventory (DSP-) in the dates chosen, one row for each line, site and date group it left, with the production and expiry dates it went with and the days it had left to its expiry when it was sent (rows in red had expired, amber had three days or less). Filters: the dates, the From site, the To site, the item, and which documents (both, dispatches only or transfers only).

Report What it shows
Transfers by Item Code Grouped by item code: under each item, every line that sent it with the date, the document, the site it left (with its marker), the site it went to, the dates, the crates and the quantity; a Subtotal of crates and quantity per item code.
Transfers by Site Grouped by the site the stock left (a dispatch from many sites counts under each of them): what left each site, item by item, with the site it went to and the dates; a Subtotal per site.

As delivered, the roles that transfer or dispatch inventory, and those that read the inventory movement reports, may run them; Value at cost shows only to those allowed to see costs.

Shortage and shrinkage reports

Under Inventory > Reports > Shortage and Shrinkage Reports and in the Report Center. Shortages count every Shortage Charge that is not voided, from a closing or an adjustment; shrinkage counts the adjustments with the reason Shrinkage and those made by a closing.

Report What it shows
Inventory Transfer Closing Register Every closing: the van or outlet, where the stock went back, the person, what came back, the shrinkage and the shortage, with the documents it entered.
Shortages by Item The quantity short and the cost charged for each item, with the number of charges.
Shortages by Site The shortages of each van store or outlet, with the cost charged.
Shortages by Sales Representative For each person: the charges, the cost charged, what has been collected through Receive Payments and what is still open, with a chart of what is still to collect.
Shortage Detail Every line of every shortage charge: date, number, the closing it came from, person, site, item, quantity, cost, what has been collected and what is still open.
Shrinkage by Item The quantity lost to shrinkage and its value at cost for each item.
Shrinkage Detail Every shrinkage line: date, adjustment, the closing it came from, site, person, item, quantity and value at cost.
